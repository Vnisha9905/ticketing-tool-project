# Signal Guide

Guidance for deciding what is a genuine retention-risk signal in each source,
versus what is routine activity that does not belong in the briefing. The
goal is to keep the briefing focused on what actually matters for the
retention call, not to summarize everything that happened.

## Billing history

**Likely signals:**

- Late or failed payments, especially if repeated or recent
- Refund requests or disputes, especially unresolved ones
- A downgrade in plan, seat count, or contract value
- A cancellation request that was reversed, paused, or is pending
- Outstanding balance, especially if aging or growing
- A billing change that coincides with a support complaint or usage drop
- A sudden pricing or contract change (renewal at a higher price, loss of a
  discount, etc.) that could plausibly be a source of dissatisfaction

**Usually not signals (routine, leave out of the briefing):**

- On-time payments with no history of issues
- A single late payment, long ago, with no pattern and no other risk
  indicators
- Normal invoice cycles with nothing notable

## Product usage logs

**Likely signals:**

- A sustained decline in login or activity frequency, especially a sharp or
  recent drop
- A significant drop in the number of active users on the account
- Abandonment of a feature that was previously used regularly
- A long gap since last activity, particularly relative to the customer's
  historical pattern
- Never having reached meaningful adoption after onboarding
- A usage spike immediately followed by a drop-off (can indicate a bad
  experience, not just declining interest)

**Usually not signals:**

- Normal seasonal or day-to-day fluctuation
- Stable or growing usage with no other risk indicators
- A short dip with a clear, already-explained cause (e.g. a known holiday
  period) and no accompanying billing or support signal

## Past support tickets

**Likely signals:**

- Repeated tickets about the same or a related issue
- Escalated tickets, or tickets with high priority/severity
- Tickets that are open, stalled, or reopened after being marked resolved
- Customer comments expressing frustration, considering leaving, or
  comparing to competitors
- A resolution that was applied but never confirmed by the customer as
  actually fixing the problem
- A recent ticket with no follow-up, especially if it was time-sensitive

**Usually not signals:**

- Routine how-to or informational tickets with no complaint involved
- Tickets resolved quickly with no negative customer comment and no repeat
  occurrence
- Old, isolated tickets with no bearing on current status

## General rule

A data point becomes worth including in the briefing when it is either:

1. Unusual for this customer or account (a deviation from their own normal
   pattern), or
2. Connected to something in one of the other two sources (see
   `synthesis-guide.md`).

Routine, expected, or fully resolved-with-no-trace-left data should not be
listed just because it exists in the source material — the briefing
synthesizes what matters, it does not restate the record.
