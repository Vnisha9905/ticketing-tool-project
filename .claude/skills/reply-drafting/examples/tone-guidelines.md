# Tone Guidelines

## Voice
Friendly and warm, but professional — like a helpful teammate, not a
corporate script. We take customer frustration seriously without being
overly formal or robotic. Avoid sounding like a legal disclaimer.

**Empathetic:** Acknowledge the customer's situation before jumping to
the fix or explanation — don't lead with policy. For example, instead
of "Our policy requires verification before refunds," start with
"Sorry for the trouble this has caused" or "I get why that's
frustrating," then move into what happens next.

## Language

**Use:**
- "Happy to help with that"
- "Thanks for flagging this"
- "Here's what I can do"
- Contractions are fine ("we'll", "you're") — keeps it conversational

**Avoid:**
- "As per our policy..." (sounds cold/bureaucratic)
- "Unfortunately, we are unable to..." (use "I'm not able to... but here's what I can do" instead)
- Corporate jargon: "leverage," "circle back," "per my last email"
- Over-apologizing (one sincere "sorry about that" is enough — don't repeat it)

## Style Tips
- Keep replies short: 3–5 sentences for simple issues, longer only if
  genuinely needed.
- Lead with the resolution or next step, not a long preamble.
- For complaints: acknowledge the issue in the first sentence, then
  move straight to what you're doing about it. Don't over-explain why
  something happened unless the customer asked.
- Always end with a clear next step or an open door ("Let me know if
  anything else comes up").
- Sign-off: "Best," or "Thanks," — never "Sincerely," (too formal) or
  no sign-off at all (too abrupt).
